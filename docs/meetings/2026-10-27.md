## Topics discussed
- **Design V (individual-level inframarginal, issue #8):** a fifth inframarginal specification. Same instrument (seats in program p in year t) with finer controls: students with identical ROLs exposed to different seat counts in different years. Class size for an identical ROL varies for two reasons: different PSU scores leading to different programs (unwanted) and the same ROL in different application years with different program sizes (the target variation). Controlling for PSU in the second stage helps isolate the target channel.
- **ROL sparsity:** most exact ROLs are unique, so exact-ROL fixed effects are too sparse. Proposed coarser groupings: first preference + number of universities (first attempt); OECD field classification (~10 broad areas or ~20 sub-areas); university × field; field × selectivity bracket. Closest precedent for controlling for ROL: the Daly–Groover paper (different setting).
- **Related paper:** Juanjo Mata and Josefa Viera (AEA) estimate value-added via RDs in Chile, with figures similar to those shown. Worth comparing.
- **Identification concern:** programs that expand may be improving at the same time for other reasons (confounding trend). Proposed checks: completion on the instrument at the relevant year and at leads/lags (t−3 to t+3), expecting a peak only at t; whether Z correlates with the average PSU of inframarginal students given the fixed effects; whether Z correlates with their socioeconomic status. Program-specific trends added as robustness (results not yet fully reviewed); university × year FE also tested.
- **Program value-added (issue #9):** program-level VA estimated with Mi Futuro earnings of graduates (conditional on graduating, so positively selected). Portfolio fixed effect as control (feasible set of programs given the ROL). Main finding: most VA variation is within institutions, across programs. UMSE (teaching degrees only) has the lowest VA; UAI (business-heavy) the highest. Top-1% VA programs are growing faster (likely business degrees at private universities); high-VA programs are larger on average, with wide dispersion. Proposed motivating figure: program returns vs. average change in program size over 10–15 years.
- **VA validation:** estimate the same model within the RD bandwidth; VA should line up with RD jumps for marginal students. Andrés has tax-authority earnings by years since enrollment (up to ~12 years), useful for validation.
- **Ministry of Labor data application:** draft proposal in the repo (Gantt chart, report milestones). Process is approval, then a contract between university/researchers and the ministry; university lawyers would slow it. Target: at least 3 years of coverage (18 months known to be insufficient). Goal: send the application this week.
