# Meeting minutes — 2026-10-15

**Attendees:** Andrés Barrios, Agustín Díaz, Emilio Borghesan, Jorge Rodríguez

## Topics discussed
- **Repo reorganization:** all results moved from the old shared Google Doc into GitHub repositories; data shared via Dropbox. Access to be granted to anyone not yet added.
- **Framing (high-return vs. selective programs):** Andrés proposed starting from programs identified as high-return via RDD instead of "selective" programs. Programs with an estimable RDD already have a queue, so the question becomes why oversubscribed high-return programs do not expand. Emilio: whether selective and high-return overlap is an empirical question; selectivity is observable now, returns need earnings data.
- **Design I (seats → enrollment/graduation, program-year TWFE):** strongest results so far. Negative effect on graduation from the program (8-year window); strong first stage (lower PSU and GPA in entering cohort); no effect on graduation from any university (switching, not dropout). Heterogeneity by selectivity is noisy (negative at the extremes). Log-log not yet tried.
- **Design II (large seat expansions, top 20% of positive shocks in a field-year):** graduation effects similar to Design I; clean first-stage event study; reduced form borderline significant. Field-by-year FE suggested over university-by-year. Callaway–Sant'Anna with not-yet-treated controls already partly incorporated.
- **Design III (private entry into the centralized system as competitive shock):** negative effect on own enrollment, especially with triangular PSU-proximity weights. Weaker than Designs I–II, reduced form not robust, concentrated in mid-selectivity programs. Geographic distance not yet in the exposure measure; Emilio suggested multiplying the PSU kernel by a geographic kernel and testing bandwidths; Jorge noted summing over the whole country may be cleaner.
- **Design IV (close competitors of a program with a large seat change):** not yet analyzed in depth (outputs in repo). Could identify effects for all program types. Endogeneity concern: competitors may expand for correlated reasons.
- **Paper strategy:** RDD gives marginal benefit of quality; inframarginal designs give marginal cost of expansion; simulate reallocation to equate the two. Emilio: value is in the novelty of comparing marginal benefit and cost across the selectivity/return spectrum, not in any single coefficient (analogy to teacher/school value-added papers). Andrés: a pure reduced-form framing is risky; the counterfactual exercise is what makes identification defensible. Target is a simple Walters-style reallocation exercise, not a full structural model.
- **Empirical Bayes / shrinkage** floated to get program-specific estimates from noisy designs. All designs should deliver variation across programs, not just an aggregate coefficient. Waiting-list length (queue as a share of enrollment) worth constructing as an extra program-level measure.
- **Labor market data:** letter to the Ministry of Labor drafted (José Ignacio) but not sent. PSU data unavailable at the Central Bank (Ministry of Education blocked access). Labor outcomes are the most important eventual outcome; first-stage work should be settled before the data arrives.

## Decisions
- Revisit the RDD framing in the coming weeks to align the narrative with the high-return angle before writing begins.
- Priority for all four designs: program-level estimates (needed for the reallocation exercise).
