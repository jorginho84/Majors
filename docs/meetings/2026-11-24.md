## Topics discussed
- **Balance and control variables (Design V, issue #10):**
  - Two new sets of controls were added to address the imperfect balance:
    - Program demand at t−1: first-preference applicants and waiting-list counts.
    - University × year and field × year FE.
  - The CPT controls do most of the work in reducing predicted graduation. Adding them one at a time vs. all at once has not been tested, so which CPT variable matters most is still unknown.
  - Emilio suggested controlling for PSU quantiles (5 or 10) instead of the raw score: graduation is likely non-linear in PSU, so the balance results may change.
  - The PSU scale changed around 2020–21, but all study years predate this.
- **2SLS results and interpretation:**
  - Effect on predicted graduation is tiny: +0.01 pp per 10 additional seats (about 1 pp even at 100 extra seats).
  - Graduation at *any* university (not the own program) shows a positive effect.
    - Hypothesis: more seats push students to transfer to better-fit programs.
    - Emilio referenced Tomas Larocque's paper (spelling as in the notes): transfers would need to happen within year 1 to show up in the 8-year graduation window.
  - Logs vs. levels diverge:
    - Levels: no effect on own-program graduation (dominated by large numerical increases at big schools).
    - Logs: slightly negative effect, possibly driven by proportional increases at smaller programs.
    - Emilio flagged this as speculative given the statistical insignificance.
  - University finances: seat increases at t−1 predict higher total expenses and tuition revenue at t, with no effect on per-student figures. University × year FE in the main specifications should absorb this.
- **Ministry of Labor:** the co-author at the ministry has not responded yet (busy with congressional legislation).
- **Value added:** the VA specification is to be sent to Emilio by email (delayed by a bank conference).
