# Meeting minutes — 2026-11-10

**Attendees:** not stated in the notes (José Ignacio, Emilio and Jorge are mentioned).

## Topics discussed
- **Ministry of Labor data access:** an email went to the co-author's contact at the Ministry just before the meeting. The application is a formality: there is no competitive review, only a check that it fits the ministry's mission. The process should be slow but is on track.
- **Design V (ROL fixed effects, issues #8, #10):** a 2SLS that compares students with the same ROL across years. Three ROL definitions were shown:
  - exact portfolio (most demanding; loses many observations);
  - same set of programs, regardless of rank (intermediate);
  - first preference + same set of universities (least demanding).

  With the stacked ordered list, few students share a ROL across years (singletons), which limits power. Suggestion: group programs into broader categories (field × institution, selectivity tiers).
- **Balance table:** Z (seats) is positively correlated with predicted graduation and with student-quality covariates. Most coefficients are significant but small (PSU ~0.4 points against a mean of ~600). Open checks:
  - The private-subsidized school coefficient of 0.14 means 14 percentage points, not 0.14 pp. Needs a double-check.
  - School-type shares don't sum to 100%, and the coefficients don't net to zero.
  - SEs should be clustered by program-year. Needs verification.
  - Program codes changed in 2012. José Ignacio confirmed they were standardized.

  Hypothesis: once PSU is controlled for, the remaining variation in Z may be uncorrelated with the covariates.
- **Distributed lags and pre-trends:**
  - Enrollment (left panel) is clean: seats in T predict enrollment in T only.
  - Graduation (right panel) has a positive, significant T−1 coefficient. Possible explanations: better students apply after seeing the expansion; the university prepares resources ahead of the expansion; a revenue effect (more students, more funding for every cohort).
  - This is linked to the balance problem: within a two-year span, seats correlate with the determinants of graduation.
  - Emilio suggested looking at the average time series of Z after partialling out its mean vs. X. The specification is still being worked out.
- **Value-added heterogeneity (issues #9, #11):** programs split into VA terciles (income rank as outcome, controlling for the admission portfolio). The largest effects are in the middle tercile, the lowest tercile has negative effects, and the top tercile shows nothing. The first stage is stronger for high-VA programs (~0.9). VA fixed effects are constant across years (φ_P, not φ_PT) to avoid noise from sampling uncertainty.
- **Earnings data:** Mi Futuro has three limits:
  - It gives program-level averages only.
  - It is conditional on employment (zeros excluded), which biases programs with more women.
  - It measures earnings only ~4–5 years after graduation.

  Proposal: program-level average earnings from Central Bank data. These would include zeros, cover up to ~10 years after enrollment and could condition on enrollment instead of graduation. They need a disclosure review but are feasible.

## Decisions
- Keep VA fixed effects constant across years (φ_P).
- Next balance-table iteration controls for PSU score (and possibly PSU quantiles).
